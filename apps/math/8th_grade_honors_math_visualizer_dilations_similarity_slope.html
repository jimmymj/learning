Angle-Angle (AA) Similarity Criterion

Because dilations preserve angle measures and rigid motions preserve both angles and lengths, two triangles are guaranteed to be similar if at least two pairs of corresponding angles are congruent.

NC.8.G.3 • Coordinate Rules

Dilations on the Plane

  • • Centered at Origin (0,0):
    (x, y) → (kx, ky)
  • • Centered at Arbitrary Point (cx, cy):
    (x, y) → (cx + k(x - cx), cy + k(y - cy))
  • • Scale Factor Classification:
    • - k > 1: Enlargement
    • - 0 < k < 1: Reduction
    • - k = 1: Congruent (Identity)
  • • Orientation & Parallelism: Dilations preserve orientation and map every line segment to a parallel line segment (unless the line passes through the center of dilation).
NC.8.G.4 • Similarity & Scaling

Similar Figures & Ratios

  • • Definition of Similarity: Two figures are similar if a sequence of rigid motions (translations, reflections, rotations) and a dilation maps one onto the other.
  • • Angle Preservation: Corresponding angles in similar figures are always congruent (invariant under dilation).
  • • Side & Perimeter Ratio: Corresponding sides and perimeters scale by k.
  • • Area Scaling Law: If a figure is dilated by scale factor k, its area scales by k².
    Example: If k = 3, Perimeter is 3× larger, and Area is 9× larger.
NC.8.EE.6 • Slope & Equations

Slope Triangles & Lines

  • • Why Slope is Constant: Any two right triangles formed by the vertical rise and horizontal run along a line share congruent right angles and congruent corresponding angles (by parallel horizontal/vertical grid lines), making them similar by AA.
  • • Proportional Rise-to-Run:
    Δy₁ / Δx₁ = Δy₂ / Δx₂ = m
  • • Line Through the Origin: Using points (0,0) and (x,y), (y - 0)/(x - 0) = m ⇒ y = mx.
  • • Y-Intercept (0, b): Using points (0,b) and (x,y), (y - b)/(x - 0) = m ⇒ y = mx + b.
